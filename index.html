Section 1: The Core Principles of Indian Micro-Business Cash Management

Sound retail financial management rests upon three foundational rules: knowing daily liquid cash, enforcing strict customer debt recovery timelines, and separating business revenue from personal pocket money. In traditional Kirana trade, store owners frequently withdraw cash directly from the register for domestic expenses without recording it. Over weeks, this generates unexplainable working capital shortages. Khata Bahi introduces the digital 'Gullak' module to resolve this exact friction by offering an offline piggy-bank reserve directly adjacent to the commercial ledger, complete with itemized deletion controls.

Section 2: The Economic Dynamics of Customer Credit (Udhar)

Customer credit fosters long-term neighborhood patronage, but unchecked receivables compromise store inventory turnover. When credit extends past 30 days without interest or follow-up, the probability of debt default rises above 20%. To counter this:

  • Define Credit Limits: Set hard ceilings (e.g. ₹2,000 per customer or 14-day repayment cycles).
  • Automated Interest Accrual: Use the built-in Credit Due Interest Calculator to clearly compute late payment charges when debts exceed agreed cycles.
  • Transparent Digital Statements: Generate one-click CSV statements or WhatsApp bills at the end of every week to keep parties aligned.

Section 3: Mastering Goods and Services Tax (GST) Compliance

Under the Indian Goods and Services Tax framework, understanding tax classification is vital for every retailer:

  • Intrastate Transactions (Within State): GST is divided equally between CGST (Central Tax) and SGST (State Tax). An 18% slab translates to 9% CGST and 9% SGST.
  • Interstate Transactions (Between States): The entire GST rate is calculated as IGST (Integrated Tax).
  • Tax Invoices vs. Payment Vouchers: Tax Invoices record the legal transfer of goods and allow input tax credit (ITC) claims; payment vouchers merely record fund settlement without triggering tax recalculation.

Retail Bookkeeping, GST Invoicing & Credit Recovery Principles

Operational procedures and tax frameworks tailored for Indian retail commerce.

1 Single-Entry Cash Flow vs. Double-Entry Accounting

While multinational enterprises rely on multi-tier double-entry ledgers with complex debit and credit accounts, local retail counters thrive on chronological single-entry cash books. In single-entry systems, receipts (Jama) and payouts (Udhar) are totaled against opening cash balances daily.

  • Opening Drawer Total: Verify exact liquid cash available before opening morning trade.
  • Instantaneous Transaction Logging: Record counter settlements as soon as funds clear to avoid memory gaps.
  • EOD Audit: Calculate Opening Cash + Daily Receipts - Daily Expenditures. Any deviation flags cash register leakage.

2 Best Practices for Customer Credit (Udhar) Management

Customer credit is essential in neighborhood retail, but uncontrolled credit chokes working capital:

  • Credit Ceilings: Assign clear transaction maximums per customer. Halt further credit once unpaid balances reach the limit.
  • Routine Statements: Deliver digital balance statements via SMS or WhatsApp every weekend rather than surprising buyers at month-end.
  • Early Settlement Incentives: Offer 1–2% discounts for bills settled within 48 hours to preserve store liquidity.

3 Understanding GST Invoicing Requirements

Under Indian GST compliance rules, every commercial transaction must be accurately documented:

  • Intrastate Sales: Total GST is split evenly into CGST (Central Tax) and SGST (State Tax).
  • Interstate Sales: The entire GST rate applies as IGST (Integrated Tax).
  • Tax Invoice vs. Payment Receipt: Tax Invoices record commercial transactions and claim Input Tax Credit (ITC); receipts confirm monetary payments without tax implications.
